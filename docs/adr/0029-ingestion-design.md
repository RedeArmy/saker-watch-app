# ADR-0029: Market data ingestion design

- **Status:** accepted (owner confirmed 2026-10-19)
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Deciders:** Eder

## Context
Prices and FX must arrive after each market's close, be backfilled when a stock is added, survive provider failures and be safe to re-run (ADR-0005, ADR-0022). A listing must never be fabricated or partially mixed with another source (ADR-0027).

## Decision
- **Jobs on the platform queue** with deterministic keys: `marketdata.ingest-daily.v1` (one per MIC and trading day, key `ingest:{mic}:{date}`), `marketdata.backfill.v1` (one per listing, resumable by date range, key `backfill:{stockId}:{from}`) and `marketdata.fx-daily.v1` (one per pair and day). These are system jobs (no user); a handler is safe to run twice.
- **Scheduling by calendar:** a scheduler (as `RetentionScheduler`) asks the exchange calendar for the close of each enabled MIC in its time zone and enqueues the daily job after it, with a configurable delay for the provider to publish. Non-trading days enqueue nothing.
- **One statement per batch:** the days of a listing travel as arrays and are unnested in a single `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`, so a twenty-year history (about 5 000 rows) is one round trip; the update clause is `WHERE NOT price_daily.is_final OR EXCLUDED.is_final`, so a provisional day never replaces a final one.
- **Idempotent writes:** `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (stock_id, trade_date) DO UPDATE` on the hypertable, so a re-run or a corrected value overwrites the same day. Raw OHLCV and adjusted close are stored together with `source` and `ingested_at`.
- **Cross-store order (ADR-0004):** the raw payload is written to Mongo first with a deterministic `_id`, then the Postgres rows and an outbox message in one unit of work. A janitor removes orphan documents.
- **When to fetch:** the daily job runs a configurable delay after the close of each market (default three hours, so the volume of the day has settled; verified for Twelve Data: 1.5 million shares 45 minutes after the close, the full day seven hours later), never at the close itself.
- **A day is final one fetch later:** the bar of the current day is provisional (verified with Twelve Data: low volume right after the close). The daily job stores it with `is_final = false` and the next day's job overwrites it; performance windows and the watchlist may show the provisional price with its timestamp.
- **Adjusted closes are rewritten backwards by every dividend or split.** The daily job fetches the last few days and compares one older stored adjusted close with the fetched one; when they differ it re-ingests the whole history of that listing (one request, 1 credit, up to 5 000 days). This avoids a dividends feed on the free plan.
- **Corporate actions:** a split or dividend changes adjusted closes backwards; the job detects it from the provider's actions feed and re-ingests the affected listing's history from the earliest change.
- **Backfill (milestone 2.3b):** job `marketdata.backfill.v1` per listing (key `marketdata.backfill:{stockId}`). It asks for ten years (configurable, `MarketData:Ingestion:BackfillYears`), well under the 5 000 rows of one request, twice: adjusted and unadjusted, two credits, joined by date; a day present in only one of the two is skipped and counted. A day is final once its session closed three hours ago (`SettleDelay`). An outage throws, so the queue retries it with backoff; a rate limit does not use an attempt: the job queues a continuation (key `…:w{n}`, due when the provider's `Retry-After` or a minute has passed, plus a per-job jitter) and ends, up to `MaxRateLimitWaits` (1 500) times, because the free budget (8 credits a minute) is far smaller than the number of listings and one failed attempt per refusal would park nearly all of them as dead (milestone 2.3e); an unknown symbol, a plan restriction, a missing symbol alias, a market with no provider or no calendar is recorded in `marketdata.ingestion_state` with its code and the job ends, because retrying only spends credits. A listing that is not in the catalog or is inactive is skipped.
- **Daily refresh (milestone 2.3c):** `MarketDataScheduler` (a hosted service of the worker) looks every ten minutes at each enabled market that has a calendar and queues `marketdata.ingest-daily.v1` for the newest session whose close plus the settle delay has passed (key `marketdata.ingest-daily:{MIC}:{yyyyMMdd}`: asking again, or after a restart, queues nothing new, and a worker that was down all day queues the missed day when it starts). That job fans out one `marketdata.refresh.v1` per ingestable listing (key per listing and day), so no transaction waits on hundreds of provider calls. A refresh asks again for the last `RefreshOverlapDays` (ten) before the newest stored day, adjusted and unadjusted (two credits), and stores them; a listing with no history is handed to the backfill instead. **Rewrite detection:** the final days of the overlap already stored are compared with the fresh adjusted closes, and if any differs by more than `RewriteTolerance` (relative 1e-6) the provider has rewritten the history, so a full reload is queued (`marketdata.reload:{stock}:{day}`: the key carries the day so the finished first backfill does not block it, and a listing is reloaded at most once a day). Provisional days are left out of the comparison because they are expected to change when they settle.
- **Freshness (milestone 2.3d):** a listing is stale from the moment the session after its newest stored day has settled (that session's close plus the settle delay); `Freshness.StaleSince` is the one rule, shared by the metrics and later by the watchlist's `stale_since`. `MarketDataFreshnessSampler` measures every enabled market every five minutes and exposes `sakerwatch_marketdata_listings{market,state}` and `sakerwatch_marketdata_price_staleness_seconds{market}`; the second is the SLO ("price staleness under 24 hours on trading days") and the one to alert on.
- **Corporate actions are not read from the provider in Phase 2 (observed 2026-10-17, live check section 13):** on the free plan `/splits` answers HTTP 403 "available exclusively with grow or pro or ultra or venture or enterprise plans", which the adapter already classifies as a plan restriction; `/dividends` answers HTTP 200 with `{"meta":{...},"dividends":[{"ex_date":"2025-11-10","amount":0.26},...]}` at one credit. So dividends could fill `corporate_actions` for free, and splits could not (they could be deduced from a jump in the raw close against the adjusted one). The table exists and its checks are in place but nothing reads or writes it yet: the rewrite of the adjusted history is detected from the prices themselves (above), which is what correctness needs, and a dividends reader is an addition for later, not a requirement.
- **Failures:** provider errors are classified (rate limit, transient, permanent). Rate limits wait in the queue without using an attempt (a continuation job, 2.3e) and transients retry with backoff; a permanent failure for a listing marks it with a reason and stops retrying. The watchlist keeps serving the last known price with `stale_since` (never an error).
- **Symbols:** changes go through `symbol_aliases`; a delisted listing is marked inactive and its history kept until the retention policy removes it.
- **Telemetry:** a span per job, counters by outcome, a per-market staleness gauge and the provider budget left, registered in the observability catalog.

## Consequences
- Adding a market means a calendar, a provider mapping and fixtures, not new code paths.
- A free daily budget caps how many listings can be refreshed per day; the backfill queue absorbs the rest.
